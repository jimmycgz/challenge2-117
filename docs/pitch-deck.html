4 · The Moment That Matters Most for Trust Slide 4 / 8

Why Smart Search Alone Spoils Food—and How We Catch the Decoy

If an AI sends ten crates of perishable spinach to a pantry with no refrigerator, the food spoils and the coordinator never trusts the tool again. Look at what happened on our 6-hour spinach offer:

❌ The Top 3 “Best” Matches Were All Wrong

  • All three closest matches by meaning needed 12 to 21 hours to collect—the spinach only had 6 hours.
  • Ranked #3 by meaning (Greenline Community Pantry): Wrote passionately that its families ask for “fresh greens”—and has no refrigeration of any kind.

🔎 Why We Cast a Wide Net of 80 Charities

  • Because physical rules eliminate most matches, searching only the top 15 charities left just 1 viable option (and 40 left 3).
  • Searching the top 80 gives the coordinator 12 viable pantries—so when the first two don’t answer the phone, we still have ten backups ready.

✅ Why We Pick Safe Buffer Over a 1-Hour Squeeze

  • The closest charity that technically passed (#11) only had a household kitchen fridge and 1 hour of slack.
  • RescueBroker passes over thin margins to pick charities (#50, #54, #63) with 2–3 hours of breathing room and a refrigerated van.

💬 Transparent “Why Not Them?” Answers Build Human Trust

Every Match Brief openly lists the top close matches it rejected and why. And if a coordinator asks, “Why didn’t we give this to the big food bank downtown?”, RescueBroker looks up that exact organization and explains which requirement it failed (for example, needing 36–41 hours lead time for a 6-hour pickup).

Note: Greenline Community Pantry is one of 9 invented teaching cases planted in the challenge dataset. Our automated test suite includes a check that intentionally turns off the cooler filter to prove it catches the decoy.

6 · Operational Common Sense Built In Slide 6 / 8

Four Real-World Mistakes Our System Catches So Coordinators Don’t Get Burned

In food rescue, small details ruin deliveries. Here are four practical safeguards we built after inspecting the real data:

🍼 1. Knowing Sealed Formula Lasts 8 Months—Not 1 Hour

The federal shelf-life table lists infant formula at 1 hour—because that’s the rule for an already-opened bottle warmed for a baby! A naive tool rejects 48 sealed cases with 8 months left on the box. RescueBroker detects suspiciously short table numbers (≤2 hours) and uses the donor’s sealed expiration date.

🧊 2. A Kitchen Fridge Is Not a Walk-In Cooler

Many church pantries list “domestic cold storage”—meaning a standard household refrigerator. Other tools see “has cold storage” and try to send them 4 pallets of produce. We cap household fridges at 1 pallet max and reserve multi-pallet loads for walk-in coolers.

🕒 3. Trusting Verified Pickup Hours Over Vague Bio Text

In charity profiles, the written bio sometimes says “we can be there within a couple of hours” while their actual operational record requires 19 hours notice. RescueBroker strips vague timing claims from bios and strictly enforces the numeric pickup window.

📊 4. Instant Answers Across the Whole City

When a coordinator asks, “Excluding the 9 test cases, how many real Chicago pantries actually have a walk-in cooler?”, RescueBroker checks the live database and answers in seconds: only 6 out of 183 (3.3%)—showing why cooler space is so precious.

8 · Honest Limits Today & How We Scale Tomorrow (Judge Check #4) Slide 8 / 8

What a Coordinator Needs to Know Today—and What We Build Next

A tool that dispatches real trucks and volunteers is only useful if it is completely honest about what it knows and what it doesn’t.

⚠️ Four Honest Realities of Today’s Data

  • Who Is Real vs. What Is Estimated: The 183 charity names, addresses, and tax incomes are real IRS records (plus 9 invented teaching cases from the challenge). However, every pantry’s cooler type, pallet room, and pickup hours are generated because no public national database exists—so RescueBroker always tells the coordinator to confirm by phone before rolling a truck.
  • Home Storage vs. Commercial Cold Chain: USDA shelf-life numbers are consumer home guidance, not a commercial refrigeration guarantee.
  • ZIP-Code Neighborhoods: Charity locations are mapped to their ZIP code’s center point, so census numbers describe the surrounding area, not the exact city block.
  • No Memory of Yesterday’s Deliveries Yet: Because the matcher doesn’t track past deliveries yet, the fastest well-equipped pantry gets called first on repeated short-clock offers.

🚀 Three Practical Upgrades for Day Two

  • 1. Fair Rotation & Delivery Memory: Remember who received food earlier today so one fast pantry isn’t called three times in a row while neighboring pantries wait.
  • 2. Automatic Load Splitting for Big Farm Offers: When a farmer offers 5 tons of produce and no single neighborhood pantry has a big enough cooler, automatically split the delivery across 2 or 3 nearby pantries on the same route.
  • 3. 2-Minute Pantry Self-Check-In: Let local pantry managers text or talk to RescueBroker to update their real cooler space and pallet-jack access—replacing generated profiles with verified local knowledge.
