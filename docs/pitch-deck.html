⚡ Why It Responds in About 20 Seconds (And Why We Chose Correctness Over Raw Speed)

Early prototypes that passed 80 charities back and forth between multiple AI chatbots took 76 to 86 seconds per step—far too slow while a grocer is on hold. Keeping the physical safety rules in fast, offline-tested code with Google’s gemini-3-flash-preview at low thinking delivers answers in about 20 seconds on the live service (19.9s, 22.5s, and 20.3s, one sample each). We chose that model for correctness, not speed: it passed 41 of 42 test checks against 38 of 42 for gemini-2.5-flash, which was quicker on Cloud Run (~13s) and ran SQL it should have refused in one of three runs.

4 · The Moment That Matters Most for Trust (Judge Check #2) Slide 4 / 8

Why Smart Search Alone Spoils Food—and How We Catch the Decoy

If an AI sends ten crates of perishable spinach to a pantry with no refrigerator, the food spoils and the coordinator never trusts the tool again. Look at what happened inside our 6-hour spinach search:

❌ The Top 3 “Best” Matches Were All Wrong

  • All three closest matches by meaning needed 12 to 21 hours to collect—the spinach only had 6 hours.
  • 3rd Closest by Meaning (Greenline Community Pantry): A built-in test decoy whose bio begs for “fresh greens”—while having no refrigeration of any kind.

🔎 Why We Search 80 Charities Instead of 15

  • Because physical rules eliminate most matches, searching only the top 15 charities left just 1 viable option (and 40 left 3).
  • Searching the top 80 gives the coordinator 12 viable pantries—leaving 9 backups when pantries don’t answer the phone.

✅ Why a Van & 3h Buffer Beats a 1h Squeeze

  • The closest charity by meaning that passed the filters (11th) only had a household kitchen fridge and 1 hour of slack.
  • RescueBroker prioritizes a comfortable 2–3 hour buffer and sturdy commercial/vehicle cooling over raw text similarity.

🧪 Verified by an Automated “Broken Filter” Test

Every Match Brief automatically surfaces the closest rejected matches—always including a cold-storage failure when one exists so the coordinator can see the trap it avoided. Our offline test suite even includes a check that intentionally switches off the cooler rule to prove the test catches the decoy (which caught a real sorting bug in our first version).

6 · Operational Common Sense Built In Slide 6 / 8

Four Real-World Mistakes Our System Catches So Coordinators Don’t Get Burned

In food rescue, small data traps ruin deliveries and burn out volunteers. Here are four practical safeguards we built after inspecting the real tables:

🍼 1. Knowing Sealed Formula Lasts 8 Months—Not 1 Hour

The federal shelf-life table lists infant formula at 1 hour—because that is the figure for prepared formula in an opened bottle! A naive tool rejects 48 sealed cases with 8 months left on the box. RescueBroker warns on any table figure under 2 hours and uses the donor’s sealed expiration date.

🧊 2. A Kitchen Fridge Is Not a Walk-In Cooler

Many church pantries list “domestic cold storage”—meaning a standard household refrigerator. Other tools see “has cold storage” and try to send them 4 pallets of produce. We cap household fridges at 1 pallet max and reserve multi-pallet cold loads for commercial coolers.

🕒 3. Trusting Verified Pickup Hours Over Vague Bio Text

Because charity profiles were generated independently from numeric columns, a written bio can claim “we can be there within a couple of hours” while its operational record requires 19 hours notice. RescueBroker strips timing claims from bio quotes and strictly enforces the numeric pickup window.

📊 4. Instant Answers Across the Whole City

When a coordinator asks a general question—“Not counting the 9 invented teaching cases, how many recipients have walk-in cold storage?”—RescueBroker queries the live database in read-only mode and answers: only 6 out of 183 real charities (3.3%).

8 · Honest Limits Today & How We Scale Tomorrow (Judge Check #4) Slide 8 / 8

What a Coordinator Needs to Know Today—and What We Build Next

A tool that dispatches real trucks and volunteers is only trustworthy if it is completely upfront about its boundaries.

⚠️ Four Honest Realities of Today’s System

  • Who Is Real vs. What Is Generated: The 183 charity names, addresses, and tax incomes are real IRS records (plus 9 invented teaching cases). However, every pantry’s cooler type, pallet room, and pickup hours are generated because no public national database exists—so RescueBroker always tells staff to confirm by phone before rolling a truck.
  • Home Storage vs. Commercial Cold Chain: USDA shelf-life numbers are consumer home guidance, not a commercial refrigeration guarantee.
  • ZIP-Code Center Points: Charity locations are ZIP-level centroids (and postings have no donor address), so census figures describe the surrounding area rather than the exact block.
  • No Delivery Memory or Load Splitting Yet: Without delivery history, one fast pantry wins repeated short-clock offers, and loads larger than any single cooler are safely refused rather than split.

🚀 Three Practical Upgrades for Day Two

  • 1. Fair Rotation & Delivery Memory: Record who received food earlier today so one fast pantry isn’t called three times in a row while neighboring pantries wait.
  • 2. Automatic Load Splitting for Big Farm Offers: When a farmer offers 5 tons of produce and no single neighborhood pantry has a big enough cooler, automatically split the delivery across 2 or 3 pantries and draft separate texts.
  • 3. 2-Minute Pantry Self-Check-In: Let local pantry managers text or talk to RescueBroker to update their real cooler space and pallet-jack access—replacing generated profiles with verified local knowledge.
