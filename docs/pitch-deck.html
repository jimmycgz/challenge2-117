Rigor & Judgment (25 pts) · Validating the Corpus Before Building Slide 6 / 8

Four Data Traps We Found by Inspecting the Tables First

Assuming “no SQL error means no problem” ruins deliveries. Here are four concrete anomalies we found in shelf_life and recipients—and the guardrails we built:

🍼 Trap 1 · The 1-Hour Infant Formula Rule lookup_shelf_life

Opened Bottle (1h) vs. Sealed Case (8 Months)

USDA FoodKeeper lists Liquid infant formula = 1 hour—the rule for an opened bottle warmed for a baby. A naive agent rejects 48 sealed cases with 8 months left. Our _shelf_caution flags any table value ≤2 hours and honors the donor’s sealed expiry.

🧊 Trap 2 · Kitchen Fridge vs. Walk-In Cooler _storage_compatible

Capping Domestic Cold Storage at 1 Pallet Max

Many church pantries list domestic cold storage (a household kitchen fridge) alongside multi-pallet dry capacity, and frozen goods need a freezer. We cap domestic fridges at ≤1 pallet, reserve larger cold loads for commercial coolers, and require walk_in_cooler_and_freezer for frozen food.

🕒 Trap 3 · Contradictory Bio Timing Claims _excerpt

Written Bio Says “2 Hours” · Column Says 19 Hours

We found 12 planted/generated profiles whose written text claims “within a couple of hours” or “same-day pickup” while their numeric pickup_window_hours is 7 to 36 hours. We strip timing sentences from profile quotes and enforce the numeric column.

🔒 Trap 4 · Separating Planted Decoys in City Queries BigQuery MCP

Only 6 of 183 Real Charities Have a Walk-In Cooler (3.3%)

The corpus mixes 183 real IRS charities with 9 planted teaching cases (`profile_source = 'planted'`). Our prompt and SQL rules explicitly filter `profile_source != 'planted'` on corpus counts so planted decoys never distort city statistics.

Every trap is locked down by regression tests in agent/food_broker/selfcheck.py Bias rule: Operational constraints (halal, infant, cooler) stay in embeddings; demographics never rank recipients

Finding 1 · Why Per-Offer Mean Is Higher (22% Pov / 30% No-Car)

One fast pantry (Shakespear Rose Community Table, in a 45% poverty / 52% no-vehicle tract) won 3 of the 10 offers because short-clock grocery loads reward fast pickup. Counted once per unique charity (View 2), averages sit lower (16% / 24% / 15%).

Finding 2 · Why Volume Skews to Warehouse Tracts (9% Assist)

Farm loads make up 30 of the 37.5 pallets, and only 14 of 183 real charities have a dock or room for 5+ pallets (only 10 fit 10+ pallets). Big loads go where the docks are—proving why load splitting is our #1 upgrade.

Statistical caveat: 10 offers across 68 ZIP-centroid tracts cannot separate a real skew from small-sample noise. Full reproducible SQL & methodology: agent/food_broker/EQUITY-AUDIT.md

Judge Check #4 · Limitations & Closing the Data Loop Slide 8 / 8

What Our System Can’t Do Today—and How We Close the Loop Next

A tool that dispatches real trucks and volunteers earns trust by stating its boundaries plainly—and building the right next step:

⚠️ 4 Honest Limitations Today
1
Generated Operational Profiles: IRS names, addresses & income are real, but cooler types, pallet room, and pickup windows are synthetic—so every brief says confirm by phone first.
2
Consumer vs. Commercial Cold Chain: USDA FoodKeeper figures are home-storage guidance, not a commercial cold-chain guarantee.
3
ZIP-Centroid Coordinates & No Donor Address: Recipient coords are ZIP centroids (`ST_DISTANCE` joins nearest tract internal point only; no donor-to-pantry drive time in BigQuery).
4
No Delivery History or Load Splitting Yet: One fast pantry won 3 times, and a 5-ton farm load can’t be split across three 2-pallet neighborhood pantries yet.
🚀 What We Build with Another 4 Hours
1
Conversational Pantry Intake Agent (Closes the Synthetic Data Loop):
Pantry managers talk to an intake agent that asks “Can you take a pallet, or by hand?”, writes profile_text, and runs AI.EMBED into BigQuery immediately—replacing synthetic rows with verified capacity.
2
Multi-Pantry Load Splitting for Farm Surplus:
Automatically split a 5-pallet farm load across three 2-pallet high-need neighborhood pantries and draft coordinated SMS messages—solving the 9% volume skew found in our audit.
3
Firestore Claim Locking & Fair Rotation Memory:
Transactional reservation lock so two drivers never claim the same pallet, plus daily rotation so Shakespear Rose isn’t called three times in one afternoon.
